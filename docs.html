What Squad Arena is

Squad Arena turns the newest pump.fun coins into a running competition. A server watches every coin launched in the last 72 hours, keeps the ones with real trading, and lines up the top 15 by market cap. Those 15 are the board.

Every round, the board races. You don't buy any coin. You put a stake behind one in a market, such as "which coin gains the most this round", and your stake joins a shared pool. When the round ends, the pool goes to the people who backed the winner, in proportion to what they put in.

Backing a coin also makes you part of its squad: the people backing the same coin, who can work together on tasks like memes, lore threads and explainers.

The arena opens on the Squads view, a mirror of pump.fun's own squads, which race too. The Squad race section covers them. The coins are one click away, under Coins.

Quick start

  1. Log inPress Log in at the top right. Google creates a Solana wallet for you on the spot. If you already use Phantom, Solflare or Backpack, connect it instead.
  2. Get your practice creditsEvery account starts with 25 tSOL. They have no value and can't be withdrawn, so you can learn the game without risking anything.
  3. Pick a squad or a coinPress Back next to a squad or a coin, or pick one in the bet slip on the right. Set your stake and check the payout estimate before you place the bet. The newest round takes bets until the next one opens, on the hour.
  4. Watch the raceIn Squads, the race chart shows each squad's profit. In Coins, it shows every coin's change since the round opened. A round runs for 24 hours and settles 5 seconds after it ends, and winnings land in your balance straight away.
  5. Find your squadOpen the coin's page to see its squad: who's in it and the tasks it's working on.

When you're ready for real stakes, switch the account to SOL and deposit from your wallet. The SOL wallet section covers how money moves.

The board

The board is the list of coins that race. A coin has to pass every one of these tests to get on it:

TestRuleWhy it's there
AgeLaunched in the last 72 hoursThe arena is for fresh coins
LiquidityAt least $25,000 in its poolA thin pool is cheap to push around
Market cap vs liquidityNo more than 60× its liquidityCatches clones with a fake market cap
TurnoverDaily volume of at least 25% of market capThe coin is actually traded
TradesAt least 2,000 a day and 300 in the last 24 hoursMany traders, not a handful of wallets

Coins younger than a day get their volume and trade count scaled up to a full day. A coin that's 6 hours old with 600 trades counts as 2,400 a day. Once a coin is on the board, it only has to keep its liquidity above $25,000 to stay eligible.

Top 15 and on deck

Coins that pass are ranked by market cap, which is the live price times the coin's supply. The top 15 are the board. The next 10 are on deck: they race in Top gainer and move onto the board the moment they overtake a board coin. When fewer than 16 coins pass the tests, nothing is left over for on deck, and that list stays empty.

Leaving the board

A coin leaves when it turns 72 hours old, when another coin overtakes it, or when the rug filter catches it. The filter removes a coin straight away if its price falls 80% or more within 5 minutes, or its liquidity drops below $15,000. Every open bet on a coin the filter removes is refunded.

Rounds

Every round lasts 24 hours. A new one opens every hour, on the hour (UTC), so up to 24 rounds run at once. A round takes bets only during its first hour, until the next round opens. After that it runs to its end with the bets it has, so every bet runs for 24 hours.

The field, the set of coins that race, is fixed when a round opens: the board and on-deck list at that moment. Coins that join the board later can still enter Last coin standing at its next checkpoint, but they don't race in the other markets until the next round.

How returns are measured

Every market starts from the same number: each coin's return over the round. Rather than comparing two single prices, the arena compares two averages. One covers the opening window, the first 3% of the round. The other covers the closing window, the last 3%. Each average is time-weighted, so a price that held for ten seconds counts ten times as much as one that lasted a second.

R = average price in the closing window ÷ average price in the opening window − 1

That means a single trade in the final second can't swing a result. To move a coin's score, its price has to hold through the window.

Prices come from each coin's own pool on the Solana chain, read on every swap, so everyone in the arena sees the same price at the same moment. A coin that had no price yet when the round opened is measured from its first price. Once a coin's opening window has passed, its opening average is fixed for the rest of the round.

Top gainer

The coin with the highest return wins. It's the simplest market and the only one where on-deck coins race too, so up to 25 coins can be in the field.

  • Bets close after the first hour, when the next round opens.
  • A coin the rug filter removes during the round can't win. Its bets are refunded.
  • The standings you see during the round use the same return, measured up to that moment.

Last coin standing

The board's 15 coins enter a bracket. At each checkpoint, one every 4 hours, the coins with the lowest return so far are cut. The number cut is spread so that exactly two coins are left after the fifth checkpoint. Those two duel to the end, and the one with the better return at the close wins.

With a full field of 15, the cuts go 3, 3, 3, 2, 2. A smaller field cuts fewer coins per checkpoint but still ends in a duel.

Betting on the bracket

Like every market, Last coin standing takes bets only in the round's first hour, well before the first checkpoint. The coin you back has to survive all five cuts and then win the duel. A later bet knows more, so it counts for less. Every bet in every market carries a weight set by how much of the round had passed when you placed it:

weight = 1 ÷ (1 + 2 × share of the round passed)

Wildcards

If a coin climbs onto the board mid-round, it joins the bracket at the next checkpoint and has to survive from there. A coin the rug filter removes leaves the bracket immediately.

The streak pass

If the coin you backed survives three checkpoints, you earn a streak pass. You can use it once, before the fifth checkpoint, to move that stake to any other coin still in the bracket. The stake keeps its original weight.

Hype delta

Two markets that compare a coin's price with the attention it's getting. Attention is measured as pace growth, written A: how fast the coin is being traded right now compared with its usual rate.

RoundRecent paceCompared with
24 hoursTrades in the last 6 hoursThe last 24 hours

An A of +50% means the coin is trading 1.5 times as fast as usual. An A of −40% means it's trading at 60% of its usual rate. Both markets race the board's 15 coins, and like every market, both take bets only in the round's first hour.

Most efficient

Pays the coin whose price rose the most for the attention it got. A coin that climbs while trading stays calm beats one that only rises on a rush of trades.

efficiency = R ÷ (1 + A) A counts as no lower than −90%

Biggest bust

Pays the coin whose price fell hardest while attention was rising: trading sped up, and the price went down anyway. Only coins with a negative return and positive pace growth qualify. If none does, every Biggest bust bet in that round is refunded.

bust = A × −R only when R < 0 and A > 0

Squad race

The arena opens on the Squads view: the squads from pump.fun's Squad Invitational, mirrored and ranked by all-time profit. They're pump.fun's squads, separate from the coin squads described further down. The coins are one click away, under Coins.

The Squad race is a market on those squads. The 15 leading squads when a round opens race on the profit they gain during the 24-hour round, and the squad that gains the most wins the pool. The bet window, the weight, the 3% rake and the house seed are the same as in the coin markets.

A squad's profit is pump.fun's figure at the time the arena copied it, plus each member wallet's on-chain profit since then, read through Solana Tracker. So it can differ slightly from pump.fun's official number.

Pools and payouts

Every round has a separate pool for each market and each currency: practice tSOL and real SOL never mix. Payouts are pari-mutuel. There are no fixed odds and no bookmaker taking the other side. You're betting against the other players, and the pool is split among whoever backed the winner.

How a payout is worked out

Your share of a pick is your stake times its weight. When the round settles, the whole pool minus the 3% rake goes to the winning coin, split by share:

payout = pool × (1 − rake) × your share ÷ all shares on the winning coin

Each pool also starts with a house seed: 0.1 tSOL per coin in practice pools, and 0.01 SOL per coin in SOL pools. The seed gives every coin a share from the first second, so the house holds a small stake on each coin like any other player.

Refunds

A refund returns your full stake to your balance, with no rake. It happens when:

  • The rug filter removes your coin. Refunded the moment it's removed.
  • The round isn't scored. The server has to see a round from its first minute. A round keeps going through a server restart once its opening prices are saved, about 45 minutes after it opens. If the server restarts before that, or starts up partway through a round it never saw open, that round's coin bets are refunded because some of its prices are missing. The Squad race keeps going either way, because the squads' profit history is saved.
  • There's no result. If a market can't be decided within 2 minutes of the end, for example because no coin had a price, its bets are refunded.
  • No coin qualifies for Biggest bust. If no coin's price fell while its pace rose, the whole market is refunded.
  • The Squad race has no result. If the arena has no profit data for the squads in a round, or no squad's profit moved, the whole market is refunded.
  • Nobody backed the winner, in a pool without a house seed. Everyone is refunded. In a pool with a seed, the seed holds the only share on the winning coin, so that pool goes to the house.

Joining a squad

Every coin has a squad. You join one automatically the first time you back that coin in any market, and you stay a member after the round ends. You can also join any Solana coin's squad for free from the search bar, even one that isn't on the board. Betting is still limited to board coins.

A squad's page shows its members, ranked by how much they've staked on the coin, the tasks the squad is working on and the proofs posted for them.

The rules for proofs

  • You have to be in the squad to post a proof for it.
  • One proof per task per person, and each link can only be used once across the whole arena.
  • Post from your own account. Work must be original, with no bots, no reply spam and no tagging strangers.
  • No buy or sell instructions, ever.

Other squad members can verify a proof they've checked, or flag one that breaks the rules. You can't verify your own. A proof flagged by two members is hidden.

Bet slip and wire

The rail on the right of the arena has two tabs. Bet is a bet slip, and it's the tab that opens by default. Pick a squad or a coin, set a stake, and the slip shows the weight your bet would get and an estimate of the payout before you place it. In Squads the slip bets on the Squad race, and in Coins on Top gainer. The other markets take bets from their own Bet buttons.

Wire shows round notes: cuts, big moves and results, written as they happen. While the slip is showing, a count on the Wire tab tells you how many new notes are waiting.

Logging in

Logging in runs through Privy, a wallet login service. You have two options:

  • Google. A Solana wallet is created for you the first time you log in and stays linked to your account. You don't need an extension or a seed phrase to get started.
  • Your own wallet. Connect Phantom, Solflare or Backpack. That wallet becomes your linked wallet.

Your account keeps your balances, your bets, your squads and your proofs, on every device you log in from. In the account sheet you can also add your X handle, which appears on your proofs.

Practice credits

Practice credits, written tSOL, work exactly like SOL inside the arena: same markets, same pools, same rules. They just aren't worth anything, and they can't be deposited or withdrawn.

  • Every new account starts with 25 tSOL.
  • If your balance drops under 5 tSOL, you can top it back up to 25 once a day.
  • Practice pools are separate from SOL pools, so practice bets never touch real money.

The SOL wallet

To bet with real SOL, you move SOL from your own wallet into your arena balance, and back out when you're done. The arena runs on Solana mainnet.

Depositing

  1. Open your account, switch to SOL and enter an amount. The minimum is 0.01 SOL.
  2. The site prepares a transfer from your wallet to the arena wallet. Before your wallet asks you to approve it, the site checks it's a single transfer of exactly that amount to the arena wallet address below. It won't ask you to sign anything else.
  3. Approve it in your wallet. Your balance is credited as soon as the network confirms the transfer, usually within seconds.

If you close the page before it's confirmed, nothing is lost. The server watches the arena wallet and credits any deposit from a wallet linked to your account.

Arena walletChecking…

While SOL is in the arena

Your arena balance is held in the arena wallet, not in your own. Bets and winnings move inside your balance instantly, without network fees. Keep only what you're playing with in the arena.

Withdrawing

  • Withdrawals only go to a wallet linked to your account.
  • Each withdrawal is between 0.01 SOL and 25 SOL, with up to 50 SOL a day.
  • Your balance is reduced first, then the SOL is sent. If the network rejects the transfer, your balance is restored right away. If a transfer never lands, it's refunded automatically after a few minutes.
  • The arena pays the network fee on withdrawals. Your wallet pays the small network fee on deposits.

How results stay fair

  • One referee. A single round server decides every round for everyone. Your browser only shows what the server decided, so two players can never see different results.
  • Fields are fixed at the open and saved, so the coins in a round can't change after bets come in.
  • Prices come from the chain. Each coin's price is read from its own pool on every swap, the same source for every player.
  • Averages, not single trades. Returns compare time-weighted averages over the opening and closing windows.
  • Late bets count less. The weight on every bet falls as the round goes on, so waiting for more information has a cost.
  • Missing data means a refund. A round the server didn't watch from its first minute isn't scored.
  • Receipts. Every settled market gets a SHA-256 fingerprint of its round, market, field, winners, top three returns, rake and seed. Change any of those and the fingerprint changes.
  • Deposits are checked before you sign. The site refuses to ask your wallet to sign anything other than a plain transfer of the exact amount to the arena wallet.

Risks

Betting with real SOL can lose everything you stake. Coins this new can fall 90% in minutes. Only bet what you can afford to lose.

  • Nothing in Squad Arena is financial advice. The arena doesn't sell coins and never tells anyone to buy or sell one.
  • Real-money betting is regulated, and some countries restrict or forbid it. Check the rules where you live before you deposit.
  • SOL in your arena balance is held in the arena wallet until you withdraw it.
  • Squad tasks are about making things. Anything that pushes people to buy a coin breaks the rules and gets flagged.

Questions

Do I own the coin I back?
No. Backing a coin is a stake in the arena's pool. You never buy or hold the coin itself.
Why is the on-deck list empty?
On deck shows coins ranked 16 to 25 among the coins that pass the board tests. When 15 or fewer coins pass, they're all on the board and on deck stays empty. It fills up again when more coins qualify, which usually happens during busier trading hours.
Why was my bet refunded?
Your coin was removed by the rug filter, the round wasn't scored, no result could be worked out, Biggest bust had no qualifying coin, the Squad race had no result, or nobody backed the winner in a pool without a house seed. The refunds section explains each one.
Can I bet after the first hour of a round?
No. Every market, Last coin standing and the Squad race included, stops taking bets an hour after the round opens. By then the next 24-hour round is open, so you can bet there.
Can I cancel a bet?
No. A stake is final once it's placed.
Why can't I bet on a coin I found in search?
Only coins in a round's field can be bet on. You can still join any coin's squad from search, and if it makes the board, its squad will already be waiting.
How long does a withdrawal take?
Usually under a minute. If the network is slow, the withdrawal is checked again automatically, and it's refunded if it never lands.
Are practice credits worth anything?
No. tSOL can't be deposited, withdrawn or exchanged. It exists so anyone can play.
